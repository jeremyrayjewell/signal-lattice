# Scene Y — Chroma Burst

## Reference analysis, 2026-09-24

E.C.H. / Eiichi Ishii, dailycoding - 20250911 / graphic. URL not supplied. CC BY-NC-SA recorded under the user's standing instruction. The supplied source (including a lit-cylinder WEBGL grid, camera/lighting setup, and the `rakkan` seal routine) was visible in conversation but is not copied, executed or stored here. Full visual-grammar analysis: [source-references.md](source-references.md).

The screenshot is a radiating field of tapered rod/cylinder shapes converging toward a bright, pale center-right point, growing longer and more prominent toward the frame's edges — a wide-angle, close-perspective "tunnel of rods" look — in a bright six-hue palette. Several much larger, glossy-looking spheres in saturated colors are scattered through the field. The whole composition has a hazy, glowing, softly blurred quality with faint rainbow-like color fringing at high-contrast edges, on a pale/white ground.

This source's technique (a `createGraphics(w,w,WEBGL)` grid of lit cylinders composited three times with tinted `ADD` blending, then `INVERT`/`BLUR`/`DIFFERENCE` passes) is fundamentally 3D; this project renders exclusively through plain Canvas 2D, so Scene Y targets the same visual character — radiating tapered forms, glossy spheres, chromatic-aberration-like fringing, soft glow — through independent 2D techniques rather than a literal 3D port.

## Independent implementation

Three own populations, all with bases scattered across an own grid spanning the whole canvas (echoing the source's 8x8 macro grid of cylinder clusters) and oriented — with an own independent per-element angular deviation — toward a shared off-center vanishing point (`CX=576, CY=272`), not converging at it:

- 15 large, bold, flatly-shaded wedge shapes (own perspective-illusion math: a filled tapered quad, thin near the vanishing point and wide at its own outer end, plus a subtle rim highlight stroke) carry most of the visual weight — about a quarter render in a darker, richer tone for contrast, echoing the reference's dark corner accents.
- 230 small, vivid dot/tiny-cylinder shapes (a short tapered quad capped with a small circle), radially distributed with a bias toward the canvas center, form the dense cluttered core.
- 7 large flat-shaded "planet" discs (solid fill plus one small offset highlight circle, not a smooth photographic gradient) orbit the vanishing point slowly, approximating a cylinder viewed close to end-on.

All three populations use an independently authored six-hue palette (cyan, yellow, orange, magenta, lime, purple).

Each frame, the whole field is drawn once to an offscreen buffer (the same half-resolution offscreen-canvas + WeakMap-cached-buffer technique already used in Scene M). It's then composited in layers: a large, heavily blurred copy first (an own approximation of the source's full-frame blur pass), then a smaller, crisp copy inset within a thin bordered rectangle on top — leaving the blurred copy visible as a soft color-bled margin around the inset rather than a hard canvas edge, which is what the source's `image(pg, w/2, w/2, w/1.1, w/1.1)` final composite (drawing the content slightly smaller than its blurred/inverted backdrop) produces. A couple of faint, small-offset tinted echoes (`source-atop` tint blended additively via `lighter`) are added on top for a light chromatic edge — an independent 2D construction of the same visual idea as the source's tinted-triple-exposure `ADD` blend, not its WEBGL/blend-mode pipeline.

## Motion and music

- Autonomous: the whole rod field rotates continuously and steadily around the convergence point; each sphere orbits the center independently at its own rate and radius, so composition changes clearly and visibly across the clip (confirmed by comparing calm vs. extreme frames — rod rotation and sphere positions both shift substantially).
- Bass: rod length and sphere radius pulse.
- Mids: articulation scales rod-length response to bass.
- Highs: rod fill brightens/opens.
- Centroid: rod lightness brightens slightly.
- Transient impulse: a brief brightness/length/radius flash across rods, spheres and the core glow.
- A slowly traveling subset of rods and spheres (the per-id sine "selected" technique used throughout this project) reads slightly brighter.
- Built with the project-wide `intro` element-introduction parameter from the start (rods, spheres and the core glow each fly in independently via `introFor`), so this scene is ready to use in cross-scene transitions immediately.

State interpolation reuses the established calm 0-5s, transition to active 5-11s, transition to extreme 11-20s, extreme hold 20-25s schedule. Motion .45/.8/1.15, articulation .45/.8/1.2, impulse .4/.9/1.4, detail .45/.75/1 (detail reserved for future refinement). Existing feature smoothing/decay is unchanged: fast 25/160ms, slow 350/1100ms, bass 120/850ms, high residue 10/500ms, transients 650ms.

### Revision history

1. First pass: the tri-color chromatic-fringe composite used `multiply` to tint an offscreen copy before an additive `lighter` recombination of three roughly-equal-strength passes. Two bugs stacked: (a) canvas `multiply` compositing over a fully transparent backdrop resolves to an _opaque_ tinted fill per spec, flooding the whole buffer instead of tinting only the drawn content; (b) three overlapping ~55%-alpha copies added via `lighter` summed to white almost everywhere the rods overlapped, leaving color only in the thin non-overlap fringe. Diagnosed via a debug capture comparing `reactive` on/off and adjacent frames (both matched — the render was actually a near-solid wash) and by inspecting a captured frame directly. Fixed by switching the tint step to `source-atop` (which respects the source's alpha shape) and restructuring the composite as one full-strength normal-blend base pass plus two much fainter (`~0.2` alpha) offset fringe passes, rather than three equally-strong additive layers.
2. Second pass: the core glow radius/alpha and rod brightening terms (selected/centroid/transient boosts) were tuned too high, washing the rod field and spheres toward white and losing the six-hue palette. Fixed by reducing the core glow radius and top alpha stop, lowering the rod lightness-boost coefficients, and reworking the sphere gradient stops so the saturated body color, not the highlight, dominates most of each sphere's area.
3. Third pass: comparing a calm-state frame against an extreme-state frame 21 seconds later showed an almost identical composition — the global rod rotation rate was too slow to register visibly, and spheres had no rotational/orbital motion at all (only a small positional drift), reproducing the same "not enough movement" issue flagged earlier for Scene U. Fixed by increasing the global rod-field rotation rate roughly sixfold and giving each sphere its own slow orbit around the convergence point (in addition to its existing drift), confirmed by re-comparing calm vs. extreme frames showing clearly different rod angles and sphere positions.
4. Fourth pass, after the user re-shared the reference image at full clarity: the rods read as flat, translucent, pastel-washed ribbons rather than the reference's opaque, solidly-lit cylinders with visible highlight/shadow shading and clearly separated saturated colors, and the length distribution was too smooth/continuous rather than the reference's mix of many small cylinders and a few very long streaks. Fixed by (a) replacing the flat rod fill with a linear gradient across the rod's width (bright edge / mid tone / shadowed edge) to fake lit-cylinder shading, (b) raising rod opacity and saturation, (c) reworking the length formula to a skewed, largely independent-of-position distribution (short by default, occasional long streak via a steep power curve) instead of one smoothly tied to radial position, (d) rebuilding the sphere gradient into a crisper lit-disc look (opaque through most of the radius, only a thin feather at the rim) instead of a glow fading to transparent, (e) tightening the core glow further so cylinder density does more of the "bright center" work, (f) adding a third (green) chromatic-fringe pass for a fuller rainbow edge, and (g) adding a small underlayer of heavily blurred, desaturated "ghost" streaks beneath the main field, approximating the source's diffuse invert/blur/difference artifact. Re-verified via the same debug captures and a fresh contact sheet.
5. Fifth pass: the user pointed out the rods all visibly converged on one locus, which the source doesn't do — in the reference, the source's cylinders are camera-facing and scattered across its whole 8x8 macro grid; the "starburst" is a perspective illusion (near-parallel, camera-axis-aligned cylinders project toward a shared vanishing point in a perspective camera regardless of where their bases sit), not literal convergence at one point. The implementation had rods' near ends anchored close to the canvas center. Fixed by scattering each rod's base position across an own grid spanning the whole canvas (echoing the source's macro-grid layout) and deriving each rod's orientation from the radial direction between the vanishing point and its own base position, with the rod segment positioned relative to its own distance from center rather than starting near center — so rods are now visibly distributed across the whole frame while still all orienting toward the shared bright point, matching the reference's structure. Ghost streaks were fixed the same way. Re-verified via the same debug captures and a fresh contact sheet.
6. Sixth pass: three further gaps from the reference flagged by the user — (a) no soft blurred bleed at the canvas edges (the implementation cut off sharply), (b) rods still read as too cleanly/uniformly radial rather than "coming from all directions" (every rod pointed at the exact vanishing-point direction with no deviation), and (c) circular end-caps existed only on a small, separate, arbitrarily-placed population of 8 spheres rather than arising from the rods themselves, unlike the source where a cylinder's own circular cross-section is what forms each visible "planet" when it's viewed close to end-on. Fixed by (a) adding a radial vignette overlay that fades to a soft pale bleed toward the frame edges/corners, approximating the source's full-frame blur spilling past the canvas edge, (b) giving each rod its own fixed angular deviation from the pure radial direction (an own stand-in for the source's independent per-cylinder rotateX/Y/Z jitter around its camera-facing base orientation), and (c) removing the separate sphere population entirely and instead giving every rod a lit circular cap at its outer end — most sized off the rod's own thickness, and roughly one in six instead drawing a large cap with a proportionally shrunken body (approximating a near-end-on cylinder whose visible length collapses toward its cross-section). Re-verified via the same debug captures and a fresh contact sheet.
7. Seventh pass: the user judged it still didn't resemble the source. Re-reading the actual reference image (rather than a written recollection of it) surfaced several structural misses a vignette/tuning pass couldn't fix: a visible inset frame/border with a soft _blurred color bleed_ margin outside it (not a plain gradient fade), a much starker two-tier scale (a handful of bold, flatly-shaded dominant shapes plus a dense field of small vivid dots, rather than one continuous size distribution), flatter/more graphic shading throughout (the smooth photographic gradients read as glowy where the source reads as bold and solid), and dark accent shapes echoing the reference's dark corner blobs. Rebuilt around three populations (15 bold wedges, 230 small dots biased toward the center, 7 flat-shaded orbs — see Independent implementation) and a layered composite (large blurred copy, then a smaller crisp inset copy within a bordered rectangle, leaving the blur visible as a margin). While tuning wedge visibility, found and fixed a real distribution bug: `gridBase()` picked a grid cell via `id % (GRID_COLS*GRID_ROWS)`, and since ids are sequential, sequential elements landed in sequential (adjacent) cells — wedges clustered in one region of the canvas instead of scattering across it, undermining the exact "coming from all directions" fix from the previous pass. Fixed by hashing the id to a cell index instead of using it directly. Re-verified via the same debug captures and a fresh contact sheet; wedges now visibly originate from all around the frame in every reviewed state.

8. Eighth pass: the user judged the rebuild still converged on one locus, and pointed out circles still only appeared in a "limited number of places" rather than at rod ends — and was right on both counts on closer inspection. Two real bugs, not just tuning: (a) the radius formula (`innerR = dist0 - length*k`) let any element long enough relative to its own base distance shrink its near end down toward radius 0 regardless of where its base sat, which is what manufactured the "one locus" look — the correct geometry (checked against the actual perspective-projection math: with cylinder length small relative to camera distance, each end's screen radius is proportional to the cylinder's own base distance, foreshortened by only a modest fraction, never collapsing toward the shared vanishing point) tapers proportionally _around_ an element's own distance from center, not toward zero; and (b) the "orbs" population from the previous pass was still a small, separate, arbitrarily-placed set of circles unrelated to any rod, not caps at rod ends. Fixed by (a) replacing the radius formula with `dist0 * (1 -+ taper)` for every population, so elements only ever sit near their own base distance from center no matter how long they are, and (b) removing the standalone orb population entirely and giving every single wedge and dot its own proportional end cap (about a third of wedges instead render as a big cap on a short stub, approximating a near-end-on cylinder). Also switched dots from a center-biased polar distribution to the same whole-canvas grid scatter as wedges, since the source's macro-grid has uniform density and any extra clutter near the middle should come from many differently-angled elements' paths crossing there, not an artificial density bias. Re-verified via the same debug captures and a fresh contact sheet: elements now visibly originate from and point in many different directions across the whole frame, and every element — large or small — carries its own cap.

9. Ninth pass, prompted by the user asking directly why repeated fixes weren't landing: re-examining the code turned up a structural bug that the previous two passes' parameter tuning could never have fixed. Every element's two endpoints were computed as `CX + direction*radius` (two different radii, both measured from the shared vanishing point). No matter how that `direction` was chosen — a clean radial angle, a jittered one, or a fully freeform random one (as tried in this same pass, initially, before realizing it changed nothing structurally) — the endpoint formula itself guarantees every element is a segment lying along a ray from the same shared center. Randomizing the angle only changes which direction each spoke points; it cannot stop the field from being a wheel of spokes around one hub, because that's what the formula produces by construction. Fixed by switching to a genuinely different construction: each element's anchor point still orbits the vanishing point over time (preserving the established whole-field-rotation motion), but the element's own shape is now drawn locally around that anchor — a length of its own, independent of its distance from the vanishing point, extending symmetrically from the anchor along its own (still perspective-influenced, still jittered) direction — rather than stretching between two radii measured from the shared center. Verified with fast single-frame previews before committing to a full render this time, specifically checking that segments visibly crisscross in varied directions with no shared convergence point, before re-rendering. Re-verified via the full determinism/autonomy/reactivity checks and a fresh contact sheet: no frame in the reviewed arc shows a single point of convergence any more.

10. Tenth pass: with the structure fixed, the user asked specifically for the coloring and shading to be reviewed against the reference. Re-examining the actual image for color/shading alone (separate from structure) found the wedges should read as noticeably muted/dusty and mid-value, with real cross-width lit-cylinder shading (a bright edge fading through a mid tone to a shadowed edge), while the caps and dots should stay much more saturated and vivid by contrast — a deliberate value/saturation split between the big background shapes and the small foreground highlights. The implementation had flat-filled wedges (only a thin one-sided rim stroke) at a saturation/lightness not far off from the caps, giving a uniformly pastel/candy read with no real shading. Fixed by replacing the flat wedge fill with a proper perpendicular linear gradient (bright edge / mid tone / shadowed edge) at a lower, dustier saturation and lightness, increasing cap/dot saturation for more contrast against the now-muted wedge bodies, and tuning the gradient's highlight stop down after a first pass read as too glossy/candy-striped. Re-verified via fast single-frame previews, then the full determinism/autonomy/reactivity checks and a fresh contact sheet.

11. Eleventh pass: the user provided a direct side-by-side against the live OpenProcessing sketch, which settled two open questions the earlier written-description-only analysis had gotten wrong. First, the source _does_ show real convergent perspective — cone tips visibly gather toward one bright central zone; the ninth pass's fully-decoupled local-anchor model (a reaction to a misdiagnosis of the "one locus" complaint two passes earlier) removed genuine structure along with the bug, leaving a flat scatter with no focal point. Second, the bordered inset frame added in the sixth pass was very likely a misread of a screenshot's own crop padding, not a feature of the sketch — it isn't visible in this direct capture. Third, on color: the source is rich, saturated, medium-dark jewel tones (deep blue, teal, sage green, rust orange, magenta, violet), not the pastel/candy palette the render still had. Fixed by (a) reintroducing real convergence for wedges — each one's tip end gathers within a modest, non-zero radius of the vanishing point regardless of its base position, while its wide end sits at/near its own scattered base position, giving genuine perspective depth without collapsing to a single exact point; (b) keeping dots as short, locally-anchored shapes (correct — short cylinders foreshorten negligibly) but weighting their anchor positions toward the center with a probabilistic compression of the uniform grid, matching the source capture's visibly denser small-dot cluster around its core; (c) removing the inset border/margin compositing entirely; (d) darkening the background and substantially lowering lightness / raising saturation across wedges, caps and dots for a richer, less pastel palette; and (e) revising the hue set to match the observed blue/teal/sage/rust/magenta/violet character. Verified with fast single-frame previews against the reference before committing to a full render, then the full determinism/autonomy/reactivity checks and a fresh contact sheet.

12. Twelfth pass: a further side-by-side against the live source showed the wedges tiling almost the entire frame edge to edge with very little plain background visible, against a cooler blue-tinted ground, and included a true red/crimson tone the palette lacked. The implementation had only 17 wedges leaving substantial empty gray space, a neutral-warm background, and no red hue. Raising the wedge count alone did not fix the coverage evenly — a debug preview at 50, then 72, wedges still showed a large empty angular gap on one side, consistently across nearby frames. The cause: wedge angle came from each one's grid-hashed base position, which is close to random, and a few dozen random samples around 360° can easily leave a wide gap uncovered purely by chance (confirmed by the gap persisting at similar frames both before and after raising the count). Fixed by explicitly stratifying wedge angle into even sectors around the vanishing point (with jitter inside each sector) instead of leaving it to the position hash, decoupling it from an independent, still position-varying base distance — settling back down to 48 wedges once coverage no longer depended on luck. Also added a true red/crimson hue to the palette and cooled the background tone. Re-verified with fast previews at multiple frames checking for even angular coverage, then the full determinism/autonomy/reactivity checks and a fresh contact sheet.

13. Thirteenth pass: the previous pass's fix for coverage (stratifying wedge angle into perfectly even sectors) traded one problem for another — the user immediately flagged that it recreated the "one locus" look, and correctly: 48 elements at perfectly even angular spacing reads as an obviously artificial, regular pinwheel regardless of how well distributed it is, which is its own version of the same complaint. The user also flagged the glow as exaggerated and the colors/gradient as still not matching. Fixed by (a) dropping stratification in favor of fully random, position-independent angle at a much higher count (96, up from 48) — high enough that a wide gap is statistically unlikely (verified across three widely-spaced test frames showing full, organic, unevenly-distributed coverage with no obvious regularity or bare gap), matching the source's own irregularity instead of imposing a regular structure; (b) roughly halving the backdrop blur radius and opacity and cutting the chromatic-fringe passes' alpha by nearly half, since the previous glow read as a bloom/halo effect the source doesn't show — its blur is a gentle uniform soft-focus, not light bleeding outward from bright shapes; (c) rebuilding the wedge color gradient to run _along_ the wedge's length (pale and muted near the tip, blending into the busy convergence zone; fuller and richer near the base) instead of across its width, after re-examining the reference and finding its cones read as fairly flat across their width with their main gradation being toward the vanishing point, not a per-cylinder lit-cylinder highlight/shadow stripe; and (d) further lowering saturation and lightness across wedges, caps and dots. Re-verified via fast multi-frame previews checking specifically for coverage gaps and pinwheel regularity, then the full determinism/autonomy/reactivity checks and a fresh contact sheet.

Completed: all 750 frames encoded with the real excerpt, with no browser errors. Deterministic out-of-order replay, autonomous movement and audio-response checks passed.
